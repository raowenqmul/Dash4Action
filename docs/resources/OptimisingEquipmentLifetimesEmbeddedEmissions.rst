Optimising Equipment Lifetimes and Embedded Emissions
=====

As the national grid carbon intensity reduces over time, embodied
carbon will increasingly dominate the lifetime carbon produced by HPC
facilities, and in many areas already does.  Therefore, extending
operational lifetime, thus reducing the need for new equipment
purchase is key in achieving net-zero.

HPC systems can be operated well beyond manufacturer warranty periods
(typically 3-7 years).  Once the manufacturer warranty has expired,
there are several options:

1. Purchase a third party warranty
2. Self-warrant using spare parts from decommissioned kit
3. Accept losses in equipment and gradually shrink system size over time as parts fail

   a. Essential and non-replaceable parts will need to have spares

Self-warranting a system may require training for the RTPs involved so
that they are able to innovate, replace components, and develop new
ideas for keeping systems operations.

Software may also be an issue: where systems have been vendor-supplied
with a vendor-supported operating system, there will be significant
security issues once this is no longer supported.  Therefore it will
be necessary to reinstall the equipment using open source software
management solutions, for example OpenStack.  This may require
significant staff training.  However, once trained, this is a highly
valuable ability which will significantly reduce future costs.

It will be key for the UK compute ecosystem to develop these skills,
particularly in an age where new equipment has significantly increased
in price.  It is not uncommon for HPC systems to operate well beyond
10-12 years at some sites.

Long operation of equipment will also require a change in attitude for
the many who value new shiny equipment, and see only value in new, and
not in old.  Unfortunately, such views seem to attract funding,
meaning that there is a bias in reward: those who would be wasteful of
resources are given funding for new resources.

Embodied emissions
=========

The best way to reduce embodied emissions is to buy less.  This can be
achieved by designing for research goals rather than a set price, and
being prepared to not use all the money you are give, if it is not
required.  However, we note that handing money back at the end of a
financial year seems to be counter-cultural, even though it is
ethical.

When purchasing equipment, the type of equipment can also affect
embodied emissions.  Key points to consider include:

* Do not specify unnecessary components

  * For example extra disks or LAN cards which will not be used
  * Bezels, cable management arms which are not required

* Consider using the smallest node form factor possible (e.g. 1U
  rather than 2U)

* Consider minimal servers

  * Immersion tanks are good for this
